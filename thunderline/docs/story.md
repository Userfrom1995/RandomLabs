# Story of the song

Thunderline is a night drive that ends at sunrise. A driver tunes an old
radio, falls in behind a line of headlights on a wet highway, and follows
what the chorus calls the thunderline: the lane-line hum under the tires,
the low wire of guitar and engine running the same current. Verse one is
midnight on the interstate (headlights, oil-slick rainbows, dashboard
static). Verse two pulls off for diner coffee and a jukebox, then merges
back on at the green light. The lead break is the stretch with no exits,
just the band playing over the verse changes while the miles blur. Verse
three is dawn coming up over the pines, and the final chorus holds the
throttle open into the morning light. The outro tags the title twice over
the turnaround and parks the song at home.

The hook is the title line itself: *"Ridin' on the thunderline."* Every
chorus opens on it, answers it with a steel-and-thunder couplet, and closes
each pass on a held payoff note ("perfect time", "morning light", "feels so
right"). The words were written to be sung at 160 BPM over 12-bar changes:
short stressed syllables on the backbeat, open vowels on the held notes,
and no line longer than a single breath at tempo.

Nothing here is borrowed. The title, every lyric line, the melody, the
chord grid, and the arrangement were authored in-repo for this project, and
the test suite scans every lyric against a blocklist of famous rock lyric
fragments to keep it that way. See [License](license.md).
