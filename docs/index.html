Redline Rush

A polished, dependency-free browser racing game: steer a four-lane highway at ever-increasing speed, dodge oncoming traffic, and grab fuel cans before the tank runs dry. Graphics, audio, and scoring are all plain HTML5 Canvas 2D and the Web Audio API — no frameworks, no CDNs, no build step, and it works offline from a single folder.

▶ Play Redline Rush now

Procedural everything

Cars, the road, trees, and every sound effect are drawn and synthesized at runtime. Zero image or audio assets.

Fair by construction

Traffic always leaves a passable lane; near misses and fuel pickups reward close driving and good route planning.

Tested

20 unit tests for the pure gameplay logic (node --test), plus a headless-browser smoke test that drives the real game.

How to play

Click Start Engine (or press Enter). Your car sits at the bottom of a four-lane road that scrolls faster the farther you drive.

  • Steer — arrow keys or A/D, or tap/drag the road on touch screens (touch devices also get on-screen steer and brake buttons).
  • Brake — hold ↓ / S / the BRAKE button to drop to ~55% speed. Slower driving burns less fuel but earns score more slowly.
  • Fuel — the tank drains continuously, faster at high speed. Collect the red fuel cans (yellow band) to refill. Empty tank = game over; if fuel is low a can is guaranteed to appear soon.
  • Lives — you have 3. Hitting traffic costs one life and a couple of seconds of invincibility; three hits ends the run.
  • Near misses — threading past a car within a whisker earns a +25 near-miss bonus. That's where the real points come from.
  • Scoring — continuous score for distance (faster = more per second), plus near-miss and fuel bonuses. Best score is saved in localStorage on the device you play on.

Running it

There is nothing to install — it's a static web app.

  • Deployed — open https://userfrom1995.github.io/Random/rush/.
  • Locally — open rush/index.html directly in a browser, or serve the repo root (python3 -m http.server) and visit /rush/.

Running the tests

node --test rush/logic.test.js

The gameplay rules live in rush/logic.js as pure functions and are covered by 20 Node unit tests (Node 18+). A Playwright smoke test in rush/README.md drives the actual game headlessly: boot, start, steering, pause, crashes → game over, high-score persistence, mute, resize.

How it works

1. Architecture

  • rush/logic.js — pure, framework-free gameplay rules: difficulty curves (scrollSpeedAt, spawnIntervalAt, groupSizeAt), forgiving AABB collision, lane math, score formatting, sanitized high-score persistence, fuel math, the traffic spawner, and near-miss detection. Exposed as window.Redline in the browser and as a CommonJS module for Node tests.
  • rush/game.js — the browser side: DPR-aware canvas sizing, road/scenery/car/canister rendering, the update loop (requestAnimationFrame with clamped delta), keyboard/touch/pointer input, the state machine (menu → playing → paused → over), and the Web Audio synth (engine hum pitched by speed, collision thump, pickup blip, near-miss whoosh, game-over jingle).
  • rush/index.html + rush/style.css — the shell: canvas, HUD (score, best, km/h, fuel bar, lives), overlay screens, and on-screen touch buttons that appear only on coarse-pointer devices.

2. The spawner (fairness guarantee)

Traffic spawns in batches, and the spawner guarantees every batch leaves at least one lane free, and (with a 65% bias) reuses the previous batch's free lane, so consecutive rows form gentle corridors instead of dead-end walls. Vertical spacing between batches always exceeds a car length, so there is always a path through. This is unit-tested and validated by a simulation where a lane-choosing bot never collides across an hour of traffic.

3. Difficulty curve

Speed and traffic density ramp over distance using a clamped smoothstep, so early runs are gentle and things get frantic gradually. Braking trades score-per-second and fuel burn for breathing room.

Design notes

  • Why /rush/ and not the site root? The repo root index.html is the Random landing page, which is preserved; the game is served from its own directory. pages.yml stages rush/ into the Pages artifact alongside the landing page and docs.
  • No assets, ever — cars are gradient rounded rects with windshields and lights; the road is a gradient with scrolling dashes and edge lines; trees are procedural; fuel cans are a red can with a yellow band and a glow; audio is synthesized. This keeps the game tiny and fully offline.
  • Robust by construction — high scores are clamped to safe non-negative integers, localStorage access is wrapped so private browsing can't crash the game, delta time is clamped so a background tab can't cause a teleport, and the game auto-pauses when the tab loses focus.
