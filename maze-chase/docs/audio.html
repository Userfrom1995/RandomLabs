Sound identity and mix

One authored sound world shared by the terminal, desktop, and browser layers. The shared score data, effect recipes, and mix defaults live in crates/audio/src/score.rs; each layer voices them with its own synth (the voice timbre tables and bus ceilings live in crates/desktop/src/sound.rs and web/maze-chase.js), all synthesized at runtime from data: no samples, audio files, or external sound sources ship anywhere.

Musical identity

The score is pond-at-dusk: a mallet lead over still water, a quiet reed counterline, and small water-drop accents. Everything derives from one tempo, TEMPO = 108 BPM. Swing is applied to consecutive same-track eighth notes in pairs (long-short) without changing the bar length, so the three layers hear the same groove.

Five original tunes cover the whole arc:

  • TITLE_LOOP: three tracks (mallet lead, reed counterline, drop accents), looping.
  • FRIGHT_LOOP: a low rising drone, a reed pulse, and a two-note fall, looping.
  • DEATH_STING: a sinking glass glissando over a low drone.
  • CLEAR_JINGLE: exactly three rising notes closing on a warm bell.
  • GAMEOVER_STING: a settled low reed cadence over a drone.

Each note carries a voice, length, velocity, and track lane. The lanes are scheduled independently, so the lead, counterline, and accents keep their own timing and loop seams.

Voice and effect bank

Seven voice families generate every sound:

  • Mallet: short sine pluck with a soft harmonic.
  • Drop: quick falling sine blip.
  • Reed: hollow triangle with a breathy edge.
  • Drone: low sine fundamental with a subharmonic at ratio 0.50 (mix 0.28), an 80 ms attack, and a 1.2 exponential decay to the note end.
  • Glass: bright sine with a slow decay.
  • Bell: warm sine pair with a long tail.
  • Pluck: dry triangle pluck with no harmonic partial, a 4 ms attack, and a 5.0 exponential decay.

Rendered PCM is peak-normalized to the bus ceiling, so the layers inside one recipe never clip; the music bus peaks at 0.32 and the SFX bus at 0.50 before the user mix. The browser sample model keeps 1.5% headroom under the SFX ceiling because its band-limited oscillators can peak slightly above the ideal waveform model; the desktop render lands on the ceiling.

Overlap between separate recipes is bounded by a summed-output stage, not by the per-recipe normalization. The browser routes the summed music and SFX buses through a master limiter (a DynamicsCompressorNode followed by a soft-clip WaveShaperNode) before the destination. The desktop scales both buses down by the worst-case active source count (active_music * 0.32 * music_gain + active_sfx * 0.50 * sfx_gain) when that sum would exceed full scale, so four loud effects plus a loop and a sting at a 100/100 mix stay at or below 1.0. The web and desktop offline mix tests pin the bound; the desktop test also pins the pre-limiter sum above full scale.

Effects are layered recipes rather than single sweeps. A recipe is a list of tones with voice, start and end frequency, length, delay, gain, and an optional per-play variation. Crumbs, for example, use a 40 ms soft pluck with slight pitch variation; the warden chain doubles the whole recipe per link; relish, sunseed, spare wick, respawn, refill, sprint, time-up, eyes, and every menu cue have their own layers.

Per-layer synthesis

  • Terminal (crates/terminal/src/chime.rs): an opt-in BEL chime, off by default, marks sharp beats. The status-line ticker always narrates the run, independent of the chime, mute, or volume. The Settings screen carries a persistent Terminal chime row; visual flashes are rendered by the playing surface from game state, so the visual twin never depends on the mute or chime gates.
  • Desktop (crates/desktop/src/sound.rs): rodio renders the shared recipes at 44.1 kHz. The sink tracks music and SFX gain separately, applies 120 ms live fades (static-screen commits settle in the input path), and schedules tune track lanes with a short loop-seam fade. A missing device opens a null sink instead of failing.
  • Browser (crates/web/src/audio_map.rs, web/maze-chase.js): the page maps the same cue, tune, tone, and track data through the C ABI (mc_sfx_tone_count, mc_sfx_tone_field, mc_tune_meta, mc_fade_ms). WebAudio schedules every tone and lane, applies the same fades, and shows a "Sound is asleep - tap to wake" chip until the context is running. Without WebAudio the game plays silent with a visible note in the HUD.

Accessibility and truthfulness

  • Every cue has a visual twin: flashes, badges, floaters, the fright meter, the ticker, and the death/clear animations.
  • No cue depends on audio to be understood, and silencing audio never hides a state change.
  • Reduced flashing and reduced motion are independent of the mix.
  • A terminal without audio hardware still gets the ticker; a desktop without a device still boots; a browser with a blocked context shows the unlock chip instead of pretending to play.

Verification

  • crates/audio tests pin the tempo, swing invariants, track lengths, voice codes, recipe sanity, layer counts, crumb length and variation, and chain pitch.
  • crates/config tests pin the defaults, persistence round-trip, and corruption handling, including the terminal chime flag.
  • Desktop tests measure offline PCM for every tune and SFX: finite samples, peak-normalized to each bus ceiling, and 0.154/0.320 peaks at the documented 80/60/80 default mix. They also pin the summed-output limiter at a 100/100 mix, a nonzero fade constant (the exact 120 ms is pinned by the audio and config suites), the null-sink fallback, and the sting cue mapping. Terminal tests pin the opt-in BEL, mute/chime gating, settings persistence, and the ticker; visual twins are rendered from game state and never depend on the audio gates.
  • Web tests pin the ABI exports, first-tone legacy views, tune metadata, and routed cues; wasm-smoke, boot-gate-e2e, and browser-e2e exercise the shipped artifact, including the unlock chip, the persisted 35/45 mix, loop-stop transitions, the 0.32/0.50 offline peaks, the summed-output limiter at 100/100, the live mix-row labels, every warden chain pitch, and the persisted mute/low-volume startup. The web session unit test pins two same-tick warden cues at different chain arms; the browser harnesses cover the chain pitches, not same-tick cue ordering.
